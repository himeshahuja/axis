A concept for the next Vexo band

Vexo Board

Write on any whiteboard and every stroke lands in your computer as ink, to a few millimetres. The band clicks ultrasound to three pucks stuck on the board, hears the marker touch it, and feels your forearm press and pinch.

How it works

  1. Where: 50 times a second the band sends a 40 kHz click and an infrared flash. Each puck times the click against the flash; three distances fix the band to a few millimetres. Echoes, always late, are thrown out.
  2. Smooth and to the tip: the band's motion sensor fills in 208 times a second and says how the hand is turned, so the marker's tip, a hand's length from the band, is placed too.
  3. Writing or not: the microphone hears the marker hiss on the board; the EMG electrodes feel the forearm press, which still works in a loud room.
  4. Take it with you: pinch to take the writing near your hand, pinch again to put it on screen. The ink goes to a handwriting recogniser.

From ink to text

The ink is ordinary digital ink: strokes of points with times, so any stroke-based handwriting recogniser reads it (MyScript's iink does, in the browser). Further out, the EMG itself carries the writing: Meta's wristband research decoded handwriting from forearm muscle signals at 20.9 words a minute, with a model that works across people (Nature, 2025).

What is left to prove

  • Real echoes off a glossy board and a body. A fourth puck would catch two echoed at once (the one weak spot in the simulation: writing without a pause, 95% within 6.5 cm instead of 1 cm).
  • Whether forearm EMG feels a marker's light press on a real board, through a band, for many people.
  • Power: 50 clicks a second and eight EMG channels on a band's battery.
